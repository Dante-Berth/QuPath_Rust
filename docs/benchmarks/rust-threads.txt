P-cores. Fewer threads on more cores wins.

Toggling SMT alone costs 1.66x the CPU time to buy 1.11x the wall clock. Memory
bandwidth cannot distinguish a sibling thread from a distinct core, so it cannot
be the explanation; contention for shared execution ports can.

The erosion, whose share is the reason two sessions were spent on it:

  config                erode CPU-s   vs 1-core floor
  1 thread, 1 P-core          1.789             1.00x
  6 P-cores, no SMT           2.073             1.16x
  6 P-cores + SMT             3.928             2.20x
  8 E-cores only              3.914             2.19x
  all 20                      4.781             2.67x

It scales almost perfectly on real cores and loses a factor of two to SMT, so
most of its 26-28% saturated-grid share is contention rather than work. On six
P-cores it is 24.3%. Eleven rectangles of four scalar passes each is pure ALU
throughput with no stalls for a sibling thread to fill, which is exactly the
shape that gains nothing from SMT.
